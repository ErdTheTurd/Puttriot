Privacy policy

Effective October 11, 2026. This notice covers the Puttriot website and the iPhone app we are building under that name.

Puttriot is a practice tool for reading putting greens. The people who make it are in the United States. This page says what information the website handles today, what the app is being built to handle on the phone, and what we do not want. If a later version of the app does something this page does not describe, that version does not ship until this page is updated to match it.

Who we are

The product is called Puttriot. The name is the product name. We have not set up a separate public company page, a mailing address on this site, or a support inbox here. When the App Store listing exists, Apple’s page for the app is the public front, including whatever seller name the listing shows. This policy still applies to the website and to the app.

We do not sell personal information. We do not run ads. We do not buy data about golfers from anyone else to bolt onto this product.

The short version

  • This website has no account, no form, and no mailing list.
  • We do not set tracking cookies or analytics cookies.
  • The host that serves the site can see ordinary connection logs, the way any public web server does.
  • The app is being built so a green scan and the read that comes from it stay on the iPhone.
  • We do not want your name, a photo of you, your contacts, or a map of where you play.
  • Juniors can use a parent’s phone. We do not ask them to make an account.

The rest of the page is the same thing, written out so you can see the edges.

This website

The site is a set of static pages: home, support, and this policy. Your browser downloads HTML, CSS, pictures, and fonts. That is the whole transaction.

There is no comment box, no email signup, and no place to upload a scan. The App Store button does not send us a click log of its own. If the listing is not up yet, the button shows a note on the page and does not navigate away. That note is drawn by the page. It is not a message you sent us.

Fonts are files on this site. The pages do not call Google, an ad network, or a social plugin to render type.

Server logs

Whoever hosts the files will keep the usual server logs. Those typically include the IP address of the connection, the date and time, the address of the page requested, the status code, the amount of data sent, and the browser’s user-agent string. We use that kind of log to keep the site up, to see that it is being abused, and to fix broken links. We do not use it to build a profile of who golfs where.

Logs are a property of the host as much as of us. A host’s own privacy terms describe how long they keep them. We do not add a second analytics product on top of those logs. If that ever changes, this section changes first, and the site will say so in plain text, not in a cookie banner that exists to wear you down.

Cookies and local storage

The pages do not set a cookie to remember you. They do not use local storage to keep an identifier. There is no advertising cookie and no “accept all” switch, because there is nothing to accept.

Your browser may still store a copy of the pages so they load faster. That cache is under your browser’s controls. Clearing it removes the copy. It does not delete anything on our side, because we did not keep a copy of you.

The iPhone app

The app is not in the App Store as of the date at the top of this page. This section describes the privacy design we are building to, so you can read it before you install anything. The shipped app has to match it. If we change our mind, we change this page and the in-app notice before that build goes out.

The job of the app is to look at a green in front of the phone and show a read: a line, an aim point, contour bands, and a pace. That work is meant to happen on the phone. A read is not useful to us as a business. It is useful to the person standing on the green.

Green scans

A scan is a picture of the ground and, on Pro iPhones with a LiDAR scanner, a depth reading of that ground, plus the motion of the phone while you walk the putt. From that, the app builds a surface and a suggested line.

We are not building a service that receives those scans. There is no Puttriot account the scan is filed under, and this website cannot accept one. The surface exists so the phone can draw the putt. When you leave the read, we do not have a copy, because it was never sent.

That is a design rule, not a slogan. If a future feature needs to send a scan or a read off the phone, for example to sync practice history between two phones, this page will say what leaves, where it goes, how long it stays, and how you turn the feature off. We will not add that quietly in an update.

A scan can incidentally include more than grass. If someone stands in the putt, they may be in the camera frame. Don’t scan through a crowd. The app is not a camera roll and it is not a place we want portraits. We do not use scans to identify people.

Practice history

If you save a putt, that saved read is stored on the phone with the app. It can include the line, the aim, the pace, the stimp you entered, which sensor was used, and the time you saved it. It does not need your name.

Deleting a saved putt in the app deletes that copy on the phone. Uninstalling the app removes the app’s local storage, subject to how iOS handles app deletion. We do not keep a backup of your practice history, because we do not receive it.

A saved putt is not a location trail. We are not building a history of courses, tee times, or GPS breadcrumbs. The phone’s own operating system may still know where the device is, under Apple’s settings, entirely apart from Puttriot. We do not ask you to turn location services on for a read of the grass in front of the camera.

What the phone will be asked for

The app needs the camera to see the green. On a LiDAR iPhone it needs the depth sensor that ships with that camera system, which iOS exposes through ARKit. It may use motion sensors so the reconstruction can follow the phone as you walk.

It does not need your contacts, your photos library, your microphone, your calendar, or Bluetooth accessories. It does not need a precise location to tell you a ten-footer breaks right. If an App Store build requests a permission that is not on this list, treat that as a reason to wait, and check whether this page has been updated to explain it. The permission list in the shipped app and the list on this page are supposed to be the same list.

You can refuse the camera. The app then cannot read a green. That is the whole function, so there is no separate “browse without a camera” mode that secretly uses it anyway.

Accounts, payments, and messages

There is no Puttriot account. No password, no email login, no “sign in with Apple” required to see a read. If we ever add an optional account, it will be optional, this page will describe it, and the app will still read a green without one unless we have a reason we are willing to write down here.

If the app costs money, Apple handles the payment through the App Store. We do not see your card number. Apple’s privacy policy covers that transaction. We may see what Apple shares with a developer in the ordinary sales reports: counts, proceeds, and the country or region of the store, not a dossier on you.

We do not send marketing email, because we do not collect email addresses on this site. We will not buy a list of golfers to mail. If a support address is published later, mail you send to it is mail you chose to write, and we will use it to answer you, not to enroll you in a newsletter.

Children and juniors

Puttriot is a practice tool a junior can use. It is not a social network, and it is not directed at collecting personal information from children under 13.

We do not ask for a child’s name, birth date, photo, school, or email. We do not let a child create an account, because there are no accounts. A parent or coach can hand a phone over on the practice green and take it back. Saved putts, if any, sit on that phone.

If you believe a child has sent us personal information anyway, write through the contact path in the last section and we will delete what we actually have. On the current website, that amount is none, beyond whatever a server log recorded when a browser fetched a page.

Parents who want a harder line can use iOS Screen Time and the camera permission. Denying the camera denies the app its only eye.

Apple and the App Store

When the app is listed, you get it from Apple. Apple’s privacy policy and the App Store’s terms cover the store, your Apple ID, downloads, and payments. We do not operate the store. The privacy nutrition label on the product page should match this notice. If the label and this page ever disagree, treat that as a mistake we need to fix, and believe the stricter of the two until we do.

iOS itself collects diagnostics under your settings with Apple. That is Apple’s program. We do not turn it on, and we do not receive a feed of it.

TestFlight, if we ever use it for a closed test, is also Apple’s. People in a test are people who accepted Apple’s tester terms. A test build is still bound by this page.

Other companies

The website is files on a host. The host sees the logs described above. We pick a host. We do not sprinkle the pages with third-party scripts.

The app is being built to run its read on the phone, without a Puttriot server in the middle. Apple’s frameworks (ARKit, the camera, the App Store) are part of the phone you already own. We do not add an advertising SDK, a crash-reporting SDK that phones home with a scan, or a social SDK.

If we add a crash reporter later, it will be described here before it ships, it will not include the green scan, and it will be something you can refuse if iOS lets us offer that switch. A crash log, if we ever collect one, is about the app failing, not about the putt.

We do not sell, rent, or trade personal information. We do not share it with data brokers. We would disclose information if the law required it, and we would say so unless we were legally forbidden to say so. We do not expect a putting app to be in that position. A scan of a practice green is not something we have on a server to hand over.

How long anything lasts

Website logs last as long as the host keeps them. We do not maintain a separate archive of visitors.

On the phone, a scan lasts for the read you are looking at. A saved practice putt lasts until you delete it or you delete the app. Settings you choose, such as a default stimp, last on the phone until you change them or delete the app.

We have no retention clock for your putts, because we do not hold them. There is nothing for us to “keep for 24 months.”

Your choices

  • Don’t install the app. The website is readable either way.
  • Don’t allow the camera. The app cannot read a green without it.
  • Don’t save practice putts. Use the read and let it go.
  • Delete saved putts in the app, or delete the app.
  • Use iOS controls for camera, motion, and Screen Time.
  • Ignore the pace when you do not trust the stimp. The line can still be worth a look. The support page explains the difference between a measurement and a model.

Because we do not have an account, there is no profile to download and no account to close. Access and deletion requests, in the formal sense, apply to information we actually hold. For this website, that is server logs held by the host, and only if a request can even be tied to a person. For the app as designed, the information is on your phone, and the controls above are the deletion.

State privacy laws in the United States, including laws that give residents a right to know, delete, or opt out of sale, still apply to us where they apply. We do not sell personal information, so an opt-out of sale has nothing to attach to. If you write to us once a contact path exists, we will answer in the time those laws require.

Security

The website is static files served over HTTPS when the host is configured that way. Use the https address. There is no password database to breach, because there are no passwords.

On the phone, the read sits in the app’s storage under iOS protections: the device passcode, and encryption the system already applies. We do not add our own cloud copy “for safety.” A cloud copy would be a second place to lose. Do not leave a phone unlocked on a bench if a saved putt bothers you. More realistically, do not leave it unlocked because the rest of the phone matters more than a fourteen-foot read.

No method of storage is perfect. We are choosing the method with the smallest pile: keep the golf data on the device that collected it.

If you are not in the United States

We are in the United States. The website can be opened from elsewhere. The app, when it is listed, may be listed on App Stores outside the U.S., and Apple decides storefronts.

If you fetch the website from another country, the connection and the host’s logs are the processing described above. We do not move a user profile internationally, because we do not build one. The app’s read stays on the phone you are holding, which is wherever you are standing.

We have not appointed a representative in the European Union or the United Kingdom, and we do not run an account service aimed at those places. If that becomes untrue, this section will name the representative instead of speaking in the abstract.

Changes to this page

When the policy changes, we change the date at the top and we say what moved, in a short note under the date, for at least the current and previous change. A quiet edit that adds data collection is not allowed by our own rule in the first section: the page changes before the product does.

The date you are reading is October 11, 2026. This is the first published version. There is no previous notice to compare it with.

Continuing to use the website after a change means you are looking at the new page. For the app, a change that collects something new will be shown in the app before that collection starts, not only here.

Contact

We have not published a privacy contact address on this website. There is no form. When the App Store listing is public, the support link on that listing is the way to reach us about this policy. Please write “privacy” in the subject so it does not sit in a pile of ordinary product notes.

Until that listing exists, this page is the notice. We would rather have the policy in public with no inbox yet than invent an address that nobody reads.
