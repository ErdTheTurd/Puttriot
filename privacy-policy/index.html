What changed: live reads are LiDAR only, on iPhone Pro from the 12 Pro on and on LiDAR iPad Pro. Other devices get the demo green and the speed tools. Puttriot is a practice tool for reading putting greens. It is made in the United States. This page says what the website handles, what the app handles on the device, and what we do not want. If a later version does something this page does not describe, that version does not ship until this page is updated to match it.

Who we are

Puttriot is made by Edward Dunn in El Paso, Texas. The name on this website is the product name. The App Store listing, when it is public, shows whatever seller name Apple has on file. This policy covers the website and the app either way.

We do not sell personal information. We do not run ads. We do not buy data about golfers from anyone else to bolt onto this product.

The short version

  • This website has no account, no form, and no mailing list.
  • We do not set tracking cookies or analytics cookies.
  • The host that serves the site can see ordinary connection logs, the way any public web server does.
  • A live read stays on the iPhone or iPad. We do not receive the scan.
  • There is no Puttriot account. Tour, if you buy it, is billed by Apple.
  • Weather adjustment uses an approximate location, rounded to about 1 kilometer, sent to Apple WeatherKit. We do not keep a map of where you play.
  • We do not want your name, a photo of you, or your contacts.
  • Juniors can use a parent’s device. We do not ask them to make an account.

The rest of the page is the same thing, written out so you can see the edges.

This website

The site is a set of static pages: home, support, and this policy. Your browser downloads HTML, CSS, pictures, and fonts. That is the whole transaction.

There is no comment box, no email signup, and no place to upload a scan. The words “Coming soon to the App Store” are a label on the page. They are not a link, and clicking them sends nothing.

Fonts on the page are files on this site, plus the serif your own device already has. The pages do not call an ad network or a social plugin to render type.

Server logs

Whoever hosts the files will keep the usual server logs. Those typically include the IP address of the connection, the date and time, the address of the page requested, the status code, the amount of data sent, and the browser’s user-agent string. We use that kind of log to keep the site up, to see that it is being abused, and to fix broken links. We do not use it to build a profile of who golfs where.

Logs are a property of the host as much as of us. A host’s own privacy terms describe how long they keep them. We do not add a second analytics product on top of those logs. If that ever changes, this section changes first, and the site will say so in plain text, not in a cookie banner that exists to wear you down.

Cookies and local storage

The pages do not set a cookie to remember you. They do not use local storage to keep an identifier. There is no advertising cookie and no “accept all” switch, because there is nothing to accept.

Your browser may still store a copy of the pages so they load faster. That cache is under your browser’s controls. Clearing it removes the copy. It does not delete anything on our side, because we did not keep a copy of you.

The app

As of November 27, 2026, Puttriot is in App Review and is not on the App Store. This section is the privacy design of that build, so you can read it before you install anything. The shipped app has to match it. If we change our mind, we change this page before that build goes out.

Puttriot runs on iPhone and iPad. A live read — scanning a real green and drawing the line — needs a LiDAR scanner: iPhone Pro models from the 12 Pro on, or an iPad Pro with LiDAR. Other iPhones and iPads can open the demo green and the speed tools. They do not scan a real green.

The job of a live read is to look at the green in front of the device and show a line and an aim point. That work happens on the device. A read is useful to the person standing on the green. It is not a file we collect.

Green scans

A live scan uses the camera and the LiDAR depth sensor, plus the motion of the device while you walk from the ball toward the hole. From that, the app builds a surface and draws a line. Devices without LiDAR do not run this scan. The demo green is not a scan of your room.

We are not building a service that receives those scans. There is no Puttriot account the scan is filed under, and this website cannot accept one. The surface exists so the device can draw the putt. When you leave the read, we do not have a copy, because it was never sent.

That is a design rule, not a slogan. If a future feature needs to send a scan or a read off the device, for example to sync practice history between two devices, this page will say what leaves, where it goes, how long it stays, and how you turn the feature off. We will not add that quietly in an update.

A scan can incidentally include more than grass. If someone stands in the putt, they may be in the camera frame. Don’t scan through a crowd. The app is not a camera roll and it is not a place we want portraits. We do not use scans to identify people.

Practice history

If you save a putt, that saved read is stored on the device with the app. It can include the line, the aim, the pace, the speed you had set, and the time you saved it. It does not need your name.

Deleting a saved putt in the app deletes that copy on the device. Uninstalling the app removes the app’s local storage, subject to how iOS handles app deletion. We do not keep a backup of your practice history, because we do not receive it.

A saved putt is not a trail of courses. We do not keep tee times or a map of where you played. The one location use is separate, and it is described under Weather. The device’s own operating system may still know where it is, under Apple’s settings, apart from what Puttriot asks for.

What the device will be asked for

A live read needs the camera and the LiDAR sensor iOS exposes through ARKit. It may use motion sensors so the reconstruction can follow the device as you walk.

The weather adjustment needs an approximate location. The app rounds it to about 1 kilometer and sends that to Apple WeatherKit for a forecast, which it uses to adjust green speed. See Weather. The demo green and a speed you pick yourself do not need this.

The app does not need your contacts, your photos library, your microphone, your calendar, or Bluetooth accessories. It does not need a precise, continuous location to tell you how a putt breaks. If a build requests a permission that is not on this list, treat that as a reason to wait, and check whether this page has been updated to explain it. The permission list in the shipped app and the list on this page are supposed to be the same list.

You can refuse the camera. A live read will not run. The demo green and the speed tools remain the way to use the app without scanning grass.

You can refuse location. Presets still work. The weather adjustment does not.

Weather and approximate location

Green speed can be adjusted for the weather. The app asks Apple WeatherKit for conditions at an approximate location, rounded to about 1 kilometer. The request goes to Apple, not to a Puttriot server. We do not keep a log of those locations, and we do not attach them to a name, because there is no account.

The forecast is weather. It is not a measurement of the green’s stimp. You can ignore the adjusted number and use a preset.

Weather data provided by Apple Weather.

Apple’s privacy policy covers what Apple does with a WeatherKit request. We do not get a feed of your movements back from Apple.

Accounts, payments, and messages

There is no Puttriot account. No password, no email login, no “sign in with Apple” required to see a read, open the demo green, or use the speed tools.

Tour is an optional subscription: monthly, yearly, or lifetime. Apple sells it through the App Store. We do not see your card number. Apple’s privacy policy covers that payment. We may see what Apple shares with a developer in the ordinary sales reports: counts, proceeds, and the country or region of the store, not a dossier on you. Buying Tour does not create a Puttriot account.

We do not send marketing email, because we do not collect email addresses on this site. We will not buy a list of golfers to mail. Mail you choose to send to erdunn+greenreader@mail.grokbot.com is mail you chose to write. We will use it to answer you, not to enroll you in a newsletter.

Children and juniors

Puttriot is a practice tool a junior can use. It is not a social network, and it is not directed at collecting personal information from children under 13.

We do not ask for a child’s name, birth date, photo, school, or email. We do not let a child create an account, because there are no accounts. A parent or coach can hand a device over on the practice green and take it back. Saved putts, if any, sit on that device.

Approximate location for weather is not a profile of a child, and we do not store it as a list of places a junior played. A parent can refuse location and refuse the camera. The demo green and the speed presets still open.

If you believe a child has sent us personal information anyway, write through the contact path in the last section and we will delete what we actually have. On the current website, that amount is none, beyond whatever a server log recorded when a browser fetched a page.

Parents who want a harder line can use iOS Screen Time and the camera permission. Denying the camera denies the live read.

Apple and the App Store

When the app is listed, you get it from Apple. Apple’s privacy policy and the App Store’s terms cover the store, your Apple ID, downloads, subscriptions, and payments. We do not operate the store. The privacy nutrition label on the product page should match this notice. If the label and this page ever disagree, treat that as a mistake we need to fix, and believe the stricter of the two until we do.

iOS itself collects diagnostics under your settings with Apple. That is Apple’s program. We do not turn it on, and we do not receive a feed of it.

TestFlight, if we ever use it for a closed test, is also Apple’s. People in a test are people who accepted Apple’s tester terms. A test build is still bound by this page.

Other companies

The website is files on a host. The host sees the logs described above. We pick a host. We do not sprinkle the pages with third-party scripts.

The app is built to run its read on the device, without a Puttriot server in the middle. Apple’s frameworks — ARKit, the camera, WeatherKit, the App Store — are part of the device you already own. A WeatherKit request goes to Apple, as described above. We do not add an advertising SDK, a crash-reporting SDK that phones home with a scan, or a social SDK.

If we add a crash reporter later, it will be described here before it ships, it will not include the green scan or the weather location, and it will be something you can refuse if iOS lets us offer that switch. A crash log, if we ever collect one, is about the app failing, not about the putt.

We do not sell, rent, or trade personal information. We do not share it with data brokers. We would disclose information if the law required it, and we would say so unless we were legally forbidden to say so. A scan of a practice green is not something we have on a server to hand over.

How long anything lasts

Website logs last as long as the host keeps them. We do not maintain a separate archive of visitors.

On the device, a scan lasts for the read you are looking at. A saved practice putt lasts until you delete it or you delete the app. A speed you choose lasts on the device until you change it or delete the app.

We do not keep the approximate location used for weather. Apple keeps whatever Apple keeps for a WeatherKit request, under Apple’s policy. There is no Puttriot retention clock for your putts or your rounds, because we do not hold them.

Your choices

  • Don’t install the app. The website is readable either way.
  • Don’t allow the camera. A live read will not run. The demo green and the speed tools can still be used.
  • Don’t allow location. The weather adjustment will not run. A preset you pick still works.
  • Don’t save practice putts. Use the read and let it go.
  • Delete saved putts in the app, or delete the app.
  • Use iOS controls for camera, motion, location, and Screen Time.
  • Ignore the pace when you do not trust the speed. The line can still be worth a look. The support page explains the difference between a measurement and a model.

Because we do not have an account, there is no profile to download and no account to close. Access and deletion requests, in the formal sense, apply to information we actually hold. For this website, that is server logs held by the host, and only if a request can even be tied to a person. For the app as designed, the read is on your device, and the controls above are the deletion. The weather location is not in a Puttriot file.

State privacy laws in the United States, including laws that give residents a right to know, delete, or opt out of sale, still apply to us where they apply. We do not sell personal information, so an opt-out of sale has nothing to attach to. If you write to the address in the last section, we will answer in the time those laws require.

Security

The website is static files served over HTTPS when the host is configured that way. Use the https address. There is no password database to breach, because there are no passwords.

On the device, the read sits in the app’s storage under iOS protections: the device passcode, and encryption the system already applies. We do not add our own cloud copy “for safety.” A cloud copy would be a second place to lose. Do not leave a device unlocked on a bench if a saved putt bothers you. More realistically, do not leave it unlocked because the rest of the device matters more than one read.

No method of storage is perfect. We are choosing the method with the smallest pile: keep the golf data on the device that collected it, and do not keep a location log of our own.

If you are not in the United States

We are in the United States. The website can be opened from elsewhere. The app, when it is listed, may be listed on App Stores outside the U.S., and Apple decides storefronts.

If you fetch the website from another country, the connection and the host’s logs are the processing described above. We do not move a user profile internationally, because we do not build one. The app’s read stays on the device you are holding, which is wherever you are standing. A WeatherKit request is processed by Apple under Apple’s privacy policy.

We have not appointed a representative in the European Union or the United Kingdom, and we do not run an account service aimed at those places. If that becomes untrue, this section will name the representative instead of speaking in the abstract.

Changes to this page

When the policy changes, we change the date at the top and we say what moved, in a short note under the date, for at least the current and previous change. A quiet edit that adds data collection is not allowed by our own rule in the first section: the page changes before the product does.

The date you are reading is November 27, 2026. The first published version was October 11, 2026. The note under the date is the change between them.

Continuing to use the website after a change means you are looking at the new page. For the app, a change that collects something new will be shown in the app before that collection starts, not only here.
